# The Dungeon Building Document

Read this before building anything in Bellmourn: a dungeon, a cave, a building, a road, a room,
a monument, even one prop placed by hand. It is the house rulebook for spaces. Everything in it
comes back to two words the user asked for: **intent** and **case use**.

> Make sure it makes sense. Make sure it looks cool. Make sure it fits the scene.
> No cookie-cutter square rooms with 90 degree corners. Less retro, more modern.

---

## 1. Intent: every space answers five questions

Before a space is drawn, write down (in the code comment above its builder):

1. **Who made it, and why?** Masons cut a gallery to bury the poor; an earthquake split the rock;
   abbots raised a shrine round a bell. A space without a maker is a random room.
2. **What happened here since?** Flooded, collapsed, looted, overrun, sealed. This decides the
   dressing: rubble where the roof came in, water lines on the walls, bones where the dead fell.
3. **What is it for in play?** One job per space: *arrive*, *travel*, *fight*, *explore*,
   *reward*, *spectacle*, *rest*. A space can do a second job only if it doesn't blur the first.
4. **What does the player see first?** The view from the doorway. Frame the hero piece.
5. **How does the player leave?** The way on must read from inside the space (light, an arch, a
   stair going down). Never make the player hunt for the exit of the critical path.

If a question has no answer, the space doesn't get built.

## 2. Case use: no duplicates, no filler

- **One of each set piece.** There is one cathedral (above ground). The dungeon gets no second
  cathedral, no repeated "shrine with pews", no second identical hall. If two rooms would be the
  same, merge them or give one a different job.
- **Name rooms by their job in the fiction**, not by a generator's kind list: the Gallery of the
  Poor, the Sexton's Stair, the Mourning Gulf, the Hall of the Founders. A name the player could
  say out loud ("the bridge room", "the bell room") means the space is doing its job.
- **Props follow function.** A workshop has benches, planks and rope; an ossuary has bones; a
  tomb has its sarcophagus. Never scatter left-over props to "use them up".
- **Every dead end pays.** Treasure, a vista, a story beat, or a shortcut. A dead end that pays
  nothing is a bug.

## 3. Structure: a spine with purposeful branches

- **Linear spine.** The critical path runs in one readable line from entrance to goal. The
  player should never wonder which way is forward.
- **A few branches, each a choice.** Two to four per level, short (one passage and one room),
  clearly off the spine (narrower, dimmer, at an angle), each with a payoff (section 2).
- **Loops only for combat.** A monument the player can circle, an ambulatory round a hall. No
  maze loops: they confuse and repeat.
- **No jumbles.** Spaces don't touch by accident. Leave rock between them; connect them on
  purpose, through a framed opening.

## 4. Pacing: tight, then open

- Alternate **compression** (passages, stairs, low arches) with **release** (halls, the chasm).
- **The reveal:** a narrow, darker approach that opens onto a big lit space with its landmark in
  view. Every stage of a dungeon ends on one.
- **Breathers** after hard fights: a quiet landing, a vista, a light.
- Encounters escalate down the spine: lone shamblers, then groups, then ambushes, then a boss.

## 5. Depth in stages

- Dungeons go **down in deliberate stages**, each a level of its own with its own material
  palette, light colour, enemy mix and depth. The player feels each descent.
- **Stairs between stages are events**: wide (4 m or more), with landings every 16 to 24 steps,
  braziers at the landings, and **generous overhead**: at least 4 m clear over the nosing line,
  6 m or more on a grand stair. The vault follows the slope of the flight; nothing hangs low.
- Steps are 0.25 m rise and 0.5 m run (a comfortable, grand slope); the player steps 0.42 m.
- Never lower than about 1.5 m under the land above (the dungeon is drawn only from below).

## 6. Shape language: modern, not retro

- **No box rooms.** Use chamfered corners, octagons, rotundas, apses, elongated octagons,
  curving galleries, angled turns (30, 45, 60 degrees), and irregular natural rock.
- **Vaults, not flat lids.** Barrel vaults over passages, domes over rotundas, ribbed vaults over
  halls. Ceiling height varies with the importance of the space.
- **Level changes inside spaces**: daises, sunken floors, monuments, balconies, ledges.
- **Thickness and depth**: openings have reveals and arch rings; walls have niches, pilasters,
  buttresses and plinths; columns stand off walls to make aisles.
- **Silhouette first**: a room should be recognisable from its outline on the map.

## 7. Landmarks and monuments

- Every major space has **one hero piece** seen from its entrance: a monument, a bell, a bridge.
- **Monuments can be climbed** by stairs, and the climb pays: a vantage over the hall, loot on
  top, a guardian, a fight that uses the height.
- Scale them to the room: a monument is 6 to 12 m tall in a hall 14 to 20 m high.

## 8. Light with a reason

- The critical path is lit **warmer and brighter**; branches dimmer. Exits are framed with light.
- Every light has a source that fits: torches where people walked, braziers at shrines and
  landings, candles on altars, glowing fungus and water in natural rock. No light from nowhere.
- Big spaces get light at their edges and on their landmark so their shape reads, and the fog
  is pushed back in them so their size shows.
- Remember the budget: only the nearest 16 flames (10 on a phone) get real lights; emissive
  surfaces (fungus, water, embers) glow for free and carry bloom.

## 9. Encounters with intent

- **The dead rise where they were buried**: out of earth floors (the Gallery of the Poor), out of
  niches and sarcophagi, among the bones at the bottom of the chasm. Never out of solid stone.
- **They guard what they died guarding**: a reliquary, a tomb, a bridge.
- **Ambushes are fair**: telegraph them (a mound of earth, a body lying too neatly, a sound),
  give 6 to 10 m of reaction space, never spawn in the player's face at a doorway.
- **Mix by space**: shamblers in open halls, runners down long galleries, lurkers playing dead at
  thresholds, crawlers in low and broken places, big ones where there is room to circle them.
- **Use the space**: a fight on a swaying bridge, the dead swarming up a monument's stairs,
  a charge up a stair at you.
- **Bosses get arenas**: round, with room to circle, cover to break line, and their story in the
  dressing.
- Hits don't throw bodies back; legs come off rarely (bosses and big things almost never);
  wounds and blood show where the blow landed, never as a whole-body tint.

## 10. Hazards and physics

- A drop the player can fall down **always has a way back up**, visible from where they land.
  Falling costs health and time, never progress, and never traps the player.
- Physical set pieces (the rope bridge) react to everyone on them: the player, the dead, blasts.
- Telegraph danger: a gap in a bridge is seen before it's reached; a broken parapet is visible.
- The dead obey the same physics: if they walk into the gap, they fall.

## 11. Clear paths

- Nothing stands in a doorway, a passage mouth, a stair or the critical path; rooms don't get
  crowded. Keep a 3 m clear lane down every passage and a 3 m apron inside every opening.
- After any layout change, load the game and run `(await import('/tools/pathcheck.js')).check()`.

## 12. Fit the scene

- Materials age with depth: the cathedral's limestone at the top, older basalt and slate below,
  natural rock where the earth broke.
- A dungeon sits under what is above it: the Undercroft lies under the cathedral and graveyard,
  so it is the cathedral's dead, its clergy and its poor.
- Use the biome packs' surfaces by name, with intent (wall panels on walls, floor sheets on
  floors). Don't fill spaces with random unused textures.

## 13. Scale (the player is 1.8 m, eye 1.72 m)

| Thing | Size |
| --- | --- |
| Passage | 3.2 to 5 m wide, 4.5 to 7 m to the crown |
| Grand stair | 4 to 5 m wide, 6 m+ over the nosings |
| Doorway / arch | 2.4 m+ wide, 3.2 m+ tall |
| Room | 10 to 20 m across |
| Hall | 30 to 50 m, 14 to 20 m high |
| Monument | 6 to 12 m tall |
| Chasm | 25 m+ across, 15 m+ deep |

## 14. Performance (desktop and phone)

- Geometry is merged per material per 32 m chunk (geo.js); build with the Builder, not loose meshes.
- Collision: boxes merged into runs; angled walls as segment walls (`physics.addSeg`), never
  hundreds of tiny boxes.
- Dynamic things (the bridge) simulate cheaply (Verlet, a few hundred constraints) and draw as
  instanced meshes.
- No per-frame allocation in update loops. Keep a phone's heap well under 1 GB and textures shared.

## 15. Process: build, look, critique, iterate

1. Write the intent comments (section 1) and the layout before any geometry.
2. Build it, load it, walk it, and capture views: the reveal of each space, the critical path,
   the map.
3. Run the two critics (a level / character / 3D-modelling veteran, and a layouts / AI /
   texturing / engine-optimisation / gameplay veteran). Fix what they find. Repeat until both
   approve or seven rounds have run.
4. Run the path check. Check the heap and frame time.

## 16. Checklist before a space ships

- [ ] Intent written: maker, history, job, first view, exit.
- [ ] Not a duplicate of another set piece.
- [ ] No box room; vaulted; has a level change or a landmark.
- [ ] Critical path readable and lit; branches pay off.
- [ ] Stairs have landings and 4 m+ overhead.
- [ ] Encounters placed for a reason; ambushes telegraphed.
- [ ] Any drop has a way back up.
- [ ] Path check clean; no prop in a mouth or doorway.
- [ ] Frame time and memory checked.

## The Bellmourn Catacombs (src/fps/catacombs.js)

Under the cathedral, entered by the stair in the cathedral office. One spine, going down in four
stages; the Undercroft above the Stair Hall, the Deep Undercroft below it.

**Stage I, the Ringers' Crypt (14 m down).** The oldest crypt, where the bell-ringers lie.
- *The Ringers' Crypt*: a domed rotunda at the foot of the stair, mourners kneeling round it, the
  ringers' tomb on a drum in its middle. Arrival; two of the dead lie by its wall, waiting.
- *Branch: the Ringers' Reliquary* (west): an apsidal chapel, a short nave to an altar in its apse
  with the ringers' relic on it (a unique ring, taken once), two guardians lying still.
- *The Gallery of the Poor*: a gallery that bends hard twice, three tiers of loculi cut 0.75 m into
  its walls (real recesses, dark at the back, the torches on the piers between them), the poor
  bound in their shrouds on the shelves (some only bones now, some niches robbed), more of them in
  the earth of its floor. Four
  graves are broken open from beneath (heaved earth, the marker knocked flat, a vigil candle, earth
  stirring as you come down): the dead come up out of those a few strides ahead. Torches frame the
  way on out of its crossing.
- *Branch: the Sexton's Overlook*: off the crossing, a lip high on the gulf's wall with the
  sexton's bench and rope; the vista of the bridge, and of the dead lying on its lit far landing.

**Stage II, the Mourning Gulf (19 m down).** An earthquake tore through the catacombs; the sexton
strung a rope bridge across.
- *The Sexton's Stair*: a grand flight down, the vault lifted over it; the dead charge up it.
- *The Mourning Gulf*: a torn rift 30 m across and 20 m deep. Its walls step down in six terraces
  (sheer four-metre risers, ledges a metre or two deep) to a narrower floor strewn with fallen
  boulders; level beds of soft rock weathered back between hard ones run along every face; the
  ledges are pale with grit and lichen, the undersides dark; over the rims the rift goes up into
  blackness (no roof to be seen). Bays and buttresses along its rims, its line kinked, pinched at its
  ends. The old galleries it sheared hang open in notches cut through the terraces, each with the
  sexton's brazier burning at its broken end (lights in the cliff, seen from the bridge), their
  stones fallen below; fungus caps glow in clusters on the low walls and the stream's banks, and the
  stream's cold glow comes up off the lowest rock. The bridge is simulated: it hangs 2.5 m at its
  middle, sags and rolls under whoever is on it, bounces, swings you with it. Three metres of planks
  are gone a third of the way over (the broken ends hang): a jump you commit to. The dead lying on
  the lit far landing come at a run once you are over the gap (the fight is on the far half, the
  gap at your back); the dead won't walk off an edge (only a spell's force throws them over), and
  one held at an edge for long gives up and goes back.
- *The Gulf floor* (fall in, or go down by choice): bones, crawlers, a pilgrim's cache; the scree at
  the west end and a stair cut along the south wall climb back to the near landing. A fall leaves
  you on your last legs, never dead.

**Stage III, the Hall of the Founders (21 m down).**
- Reached by a low, narrow passage that turns (the squeeze before the hall opens out).
- *The Hall of the Founders*: an elongated octagonal hall 17 m high, arcades of clustered columns
  round an ambulatory, a 10 m forecourt, then the Founders' Tomb: three octagonal tiers carved with
  reliefs over a moulded base, one grand stair up the south face between coped cheek walls, newels
  with braziers at its foot and its landing half way, the founders' sarcophagus
  (searchable) under a canopy of four columns and a dome on top; a guardian keeps the top.
- *Branch: the Plague Ossuary* (east): a long low vault walled in skulls, apsed at its far end,
  under a chandelier of bones; plague-bearers in a green miasma; the plague's goods in a chest.
- *The Stair Hall*: three flights, turning north, north-west, north-east on landings with braziers,
  the vault 9 m over the nosings, then a processional between kneeling abbots to the tribune.

**Stage IV, the Deep Undercroft (32 to 41 m down).**
- *The Bell Shrine*: the Stair Hall comes out on a tribune high on its south wall, face to face with
  the First Bell of Bellmourn, hung on chains (its clapper swinging inside) from the arch of a
  bell-cote on a moulded plinth with a stair spiralling up it (a landing half way); sconces high round
  the rotunda's drum so its walls show round the bell. A horseshoe of two flights goes down the wall from the tribune. Ring the bell
  (E, under it): every dead thing in the Deep comes for you up the spiral, and the grille across the
  Abbots' Gallery (chained to the bell) lifts. The bell is the way on.
- *Branch: the Charnel* (west): a pit of bones 2.5 m deep with a walk round its rim and a stair
  down, ghouls buried in it, and what was thrown in with the bones.
- *The Abbots' Gallery*: the abbots' effigy tombs along it (dark chests, the abbot carved in grey
  marble on each lid), each in an arched recess with a candle at its head, the torches between the
  recesses; the dead rise off them.
- *Cindermaw's Tomb*: the deepest place, a round arena with a sunken floor in three steps, broken
  columns for cover round its ring each by a brazier, the abbot's open tomb on a dais in its apse
  between two columns, two great braziers either side of the dais.
  He rises out of it when you come near. When he falls, the ringers' rope drops into the apse: a
  one-way way home to the Ringers' Crypt.
